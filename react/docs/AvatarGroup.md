A stacked set of avatars that represents a group of users at a glance, with an overflow count for larger groups.

The avatar group is used when we want to convey that a feature, page, or section has a direct impact on a specific, identifiable group of users. It stacks several avatars together so the set of people reads as a single unit, rather than as separate individuals.

It is ideal for communicating shared ownership, participation, or membership —who is involved in something— without listing every person in full. When the group is larger than the visible slots, an overflow indicator (e.g. "+3") keeps the layout compact while still signaling the real size. Two sizes are available to fit denser or more prominent contexts.

## When to use
- Showing shared ownership or participation (members of a project, participants in a thread, collaborators on a file).
- Conveying that something affects or involves a group of people as a whole.
- Presenting a collection of users compactly where the set matters more than each individual.
- Hinting at group size while saving space, thanks to the overflow count.

## When not to use
- When individual identity matters and users must recognize or act on each person — show a full list.
- For a single user — use a plain Avatar.
- When users need to interact with specific members (message, remove, view profile) directly from the stack.
- Representing brands or entities rather than people.
